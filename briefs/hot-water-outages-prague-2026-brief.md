## Meta Description

## TL;DR Draft
> **TL;DR:** If your hot water vanished in summer, it is almost certainly a planned district-heating shutdown for maintenance, legal and temporary. By Czech law (Decree 194/2007) these outages cannot exceed 14 days and must be announced at least 10 days in advance. Check your date on the Pražská teplárenská outage map. If nothing was announced, or it drags past the notice, it is a fault, call your správce or a plumber.

### H2: Why does Prague shut off hot water every summer? (300–400 words)
- **Answer-first**: Roughly one third of Czech dwellings get heat and hot water from centralized district heating rather than an in-flat boiler (Census 2021, Czech Statistical Office); in Prague the share is far higher. Summer is the only window to service those shared pipes, so suppliers drain and inspect them.
- Cover: what "district heating" (dálkové teplo / CZT) means, why there is no boiler in your flat, why it is a whole-building/whole-street event.
- **Image**: district heating pipes / plant.
- **[UNIQUE INSIGHT]** this is infrastructure, not your landlord being cheap.

### H2: How long can the outage legally last? (300–400 words)
- **Answer-first**: Planned summer maintenance outages are capped at 14 days and must be announced to every end consumer at least 10 days ahead (Decree 194/2007 Sb.). Most Prague outages run only 3–7 days.
- Cover: the 14-day maximum, the 10-day notice rule, the fact that planned work may only happen outside the heating season.
- **Infographic**: simple "your rights" callout card — 45–60°C at the tap year-round, delivery at least 06:00–22:00 daily, 14-day max planned outage, 10-day notice.
- **Key stat**: 45–60°C required tap temperature (Decree 194/2007).

### H2: How do I find the shutdown date for my address? (300–400 words)
- **Answer-first**: Prague's main supplier, Pražská teplárenská (PTAS), publishes an address-searchable outage map; Veolia Energie Praha and ČEZ publish their own schedules. Your building notice board (or správce) will also post it.
- Cover: step-by-step — identify your supplier, search your address, note the return date, check with neighbours because dates vary street by street.
- **Table/Infographic**: 2026 Prague outage calendar (example districts + dates from published schedules).

### H2: Scheduled outage or actual breakdown? How to tell (300–400 words) [DIFFERENTIATOR]
- **Answer-first**: If it was announced 10+ days ago, is summer, and hits the whole building, it is scheduled, just wait. If nothing was announced, it is winter, only your flat is affected, or it runs past the promised date, treat it as a fault.
- Cover: a short decision tree; note January 2026, when a genuine fault left around 11,000 Prague 4 households without heat and hot water, that is what a real outage looks like.

### H2: Who do I call, and in what order? (300–400 words)
- **Answer-first**: For a *scheduled* outage, no one, it returns on the posted date. For a *fault*: first your building manager (správce / SVJ), who reports it to the supplier; for anything inside your own flat (your own water heater, a leak, a mixer tap), you need a plumber, not the teplárna.
- Cover: správce vs. supplier vs. private tradesperson, and the language reality — most Prague plumbers work in Czech.

### H2: How to survive a few days without hot water (200–300 words)
- **Answer-first**: Outages are short; a little planning removes most of the pain.
- Cover: heat water on the stove/kettle for a quick wash, shower at a gym or pool, shift showers to work hours, switch the mixer to cold so you are not billed for drawing through the hot circuit.
