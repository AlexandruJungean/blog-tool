## Meta Description
Landlord says it's your problem? Czech law draws a hard line between tenant "minor repairs" (capped at 1,500 CZK) and landlord duties. Know the rule before you pay.

## TL;DR Draft
> **TL;DR:** Czech law (Civil Code §2205 and §2257, with thresholds set by Government Regulation 308/2015 Sb.) splits apartment repairs into two buckets: tenants pay for "běžná údržba" and "drobné opravy" (minor repairs, capped at 1,500 CZK per repair and 150 CZK per m² per year as of 2026); landlords pay for everything else and must fix defects "without undue delay" once notified. Knowing which bucket a repair falls into is the difference between a fair split and paying for something that was never yours to pay.

### H2: What is the legal basis for splitting repairs in Czech rentals? (250–350 words)
- **Answer-first**: Czech Civil Code (zákon č. 89/2012 Sb.) §2205 obliges the landlord to keep the flat fit for its purpose; §2257 obliges the tenant to perform and pay for "běžná údržba" (ordinary maintenance) and "drobné opravy" (minor repairs). Government Regulation 308/2015 Sb. defines exactly what counts as each.
- Cover: why this two-tier split exists, why a lease clause can't override it (protection of the weaker party — a clause shifting riser-pipe or major-system repairs onto the tenant is void).

### H2: What counts as a "minor repair" you have to pay for? (350–450 words)
- **Answer-first**: Minor repairs are capped at 1,500 CZK per repair and 150 CZK per m² of floor area per year (as of 1 January 2026, up from 1,000 CZK / 100 CZK/m²), per Government Regulation 308/2015 Sb. §4.
- Cover: the concrete example list — light switches, door and window handles, seals, taps/mixer fittings, sockets, doorbells, circuit breakers, drain unblocking, small kitchen/bathroom fixture repairs — plus the *new* 2026 addition: tenants must also arrange and pay for periodic inspection/cleaning of solid-fuel, gas and electric heaters, flues, and etážové topení boilers.
- **Infographic**: "Tenant repair limits" callout (1,500 CZK per repair / 150 CZK/m² annual cap / examples).

### H2: What is the landlord legally required to fix? (300–400 words)

### FAQ (4–5)
1. Who pays for a broken washing machine in a Czech rental? — Depends on repair cost: under 1,500 CZK is a tenant "minor repair" only if it's on the defined list (it typically isn't for major appliances); most washing machine repairs are the landlord's cost.
2. What's the CZK limit for tenant-paid repairs in 2026? — 1,500 CZK per repair, 150 CZK/m² total per year (Government Regulation 308/2015 Sb., updated 1 January 2026).
3. Can my lease say I have to pay for everything? — No; a clause shifting the landlord's legal repair duties onto the tenant is void, because residential tenants are protected as the weaker party under Czech law.
4. What does "without undue delay" mean in practice? — Not a fixed number of days in the statute; treated case by case, but documented non-response over one to two weeks for a non-emergency issue, or immediately for something urgent (no heat, no water), is grounds to act.
5. Do I need Czech to get a repair done? — No; platforms like Tool Connect translate both directions automatically, so you write in English and the provider replies in Czech, translated back to you.
