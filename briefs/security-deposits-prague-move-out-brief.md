## Key Takeaways Draft
> **Key Takeaways**
> - A landlord can legally require a deposit of up to 3 months' rent, but 1 to 2 months is far more common in practice (Civil Code §2254, expats.cz, 2026)
> - There's no fixed statutory deadline, but established practice and case law point to about one month after handover as the norm (portalobydleni.cz, pravopronajimatele.cz, 2026)
> - A landlord can only deduct provable claims, unpaid rent or documented damage, never normal wear and tear (vejmelkova.cz, czechpoint101.com, 2026)
> - If the deposit is returned late, tenants are entitled to statutory default interest of 11.75% a year (portalobydleni.cz, 2026)

### H2: How much can a landlord legally require as a deposit? (250-300 words)
- Answer-first: up to 3 months' rent under Civil Code §2254, though 1 to 2 months is the norm in practice ([expats.cz](https://www.expats.cz/czech-news/article/tenants-rights), 2026)
- Cover: deposit vs. first and last month's rent (different things, often confused), what the deposit is meant to secure (unpaid rent, utilities, damage)
- **Image**: hands exchanging keys or signing a lease document

### H2: How long does the landlord have to return it? (300-350 words)
- Answer-first: no fixed statutory deadline exists, but established practice and case law treat about one month after handover as the expected norm (portalobydleni.cz, pravopronajimatele.cz, 2026)
- Cover: what "handover" actually means (the date you return the keys and sign the handover protocol), why getting that date in writing matters
- **Infographic 1**: simple timeline visual, handover day to expected return, with the interest-on-delay point marked

### H2: What can actually be deducted from the deposit? (350-400 words)
- Answer-first: only provable claims, unpaid rent, unpaid utility charges, or documented damage beyond normal wear and tear, not arbitrary amounts ([vejmelkova.cz](https://www.vejmelkova.cz/en/rental-deposit-czech-republic/), [czechpoint101.com](https://www.czechpoint101.com/landlord-tenant-laws-in-czech-republic-part-2-damage-deposits-pets-access-locks-business/), 2026)
- Cover: the normal wear and tear distinction (a landlord can't charge for a worn carpet or small scuffs from years of living there), the importance of the original handover protocol as the comparison point
- **Key stat**: deduction limits with source

### H2: How do you protect yourself before you even move out? (300-350 words)
- Answer-first: the single best protection is a signed move-out protocol with photos and meter readings, mirroring the move-in protocol from day one
- Cover: reference the existing move-in checklist article as the natural counterpart, walk through, clean, photograph, document
- **Infographic 2**: a short move-out checklist (clean thoroughly, photograph condition, confirm meter readings, get a signed protocol, agree on a return date in writing)
- **Internal link**: to `first-30-days-prague-flat-checklist-2026`, since that article covers the move-in handover protocol this one mirrors on the way out

### H2: What if the landlord won't return the deposit? (250-300 words)
- Answer-first: unreasonable retention is a legal dispute the tenant can pursue, including statutory default interest of 11.75% a year for late return (portalobydleni.cz, 2026)
- Cover: practical first steps (written request, referencing the protocol), when it's worth involving a lawyer or small claims process, keep this realistic and not alarmist
