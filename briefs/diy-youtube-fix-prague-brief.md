### H2: Repairs That Legally Require a Professional in the Czech Republic (300-350 words)
- **Answer-first**: Czech law requires licensed professionals for electrical work, gas appliances, boiler servicing, and structural modifications — doing these yourself is not just risky, it can void your building insurance and create liability if you rent.
- Cover: Electrical (revizní technik), gas (plynař with atestace), boiler service (annual legal requirement), structural changes (stavební povolení)
- **Key stat**: Czech building insurance typically excludes damage caused by unlicensed work
- Note: This is especially important for expat renters — if something goes wrong, the landlord can hold you liable

### FAQ Section (3-4 items)
1. Can I legally do electrical work myself in my Prague apartment?
   — Answer: No, in the Czech Republic all electrical installations must be signed off by a licensed revizní technik (electrical inspector). DIY electrical work can void your home insurance and create liability. Hire a licensed electrician.
2. How do I explain a repair problem if I don't speak Czech?
   — Answer: Photos and videos work well in initial messages. On a platform like Tool Connect you write in English, the provider reads it in Czech, and their reply is translated back to English automatically, so neither side needs the other's language.
3. What's a fair hourly rate for a handyman in Prague in 2026?
   — Answer: Expect 600-900 CZK per hour for general handyman work, with higher rates for licensed trades like plumbing or electrical (800-1,200 CZK/hour). Most providers charge a minimum call-out fee of 1-2 hours. (Cross-link to what-services-cost-prague-2026)
4. Is it worth buying tools if I'm only in Prague for a year or two?
   — Answer: For basic tools (screwdrivers, pliers, a drill), yes — you'll use them. For specialist equipment (tile cutters, pipe benders), renting or hiring a professional is almost always cheaper than buying for one job.
