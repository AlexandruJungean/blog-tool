## TL;DR Draft
> **TL;DR:** Foreigners in Czechia must report their residence address within 3 working days of moving in, unless the landlord already did it via the UBYPORT system (Police of the Czech Republic); missing this carries a fine up to 3,000 CZK. Beyond that, the real first month is about a written handover protocol with meter readings, transferring utilities into your name, and a few small but easy-to-miss setup steps.

## Information Gain Opportunities

### H2: What has to happen in the first 3 working days? (300–400 words)
- **Answer-first**: Foreigners must report their place of residence to the Police of the Czech Republic within 3 working days of moving in, unless the landlord or accommodation provider already filed it through the UBYPORT electronic system on the tenant's behalf (Police of the Czech Republic).
- Cover: who typically handles this (ask the landlord first, since many already use UBYPORT), what happens if neither party does it (fine up to 3,000 CZK), what to bring if filing yourself.
- **Image**: a calm moving-in / unpacking scene.

### H2: What should the handover protocol actually cover? (350–450 words)
- **Answer-first**: A written handover protocol (předávací protokol) recording meter readings, serial numbers, the date, and both parties' details is what protects a tenant from being billed for a previous tenant's consumption, and from disputes over pre-existing damage.
- Cover: electricity and gas meter readings specifically, why photos aren't a substitute for a signed document, how this connects directly to the tenant-vs-landlord repair split (link to the apartment maintenance article).
- **Infographic**: "What your handover protocol needs" checklist card (meter readings, serial numbers, date, signatures, existing damage noted).

### H2: How do I transfer utilities into my name? (300–400 words)
- **Answer-first**: Transferring an existing electricity or gas contract typically takes 10 to 30 days and needs the handover protocol's meter readings, proof of your right to the flat (the lease), and often a landlord's power of attorney if the contract stays with the same supplier.
- Cover: distributor vs supplier (you can usually keep the existing supplier or switch), what happens if you don't transfer promptly, the practical tip of doing this in week one since it's the slowest-moving item on the list.

### H2: Is a smoke detector required in my flat? (250–350 words)
- **Answer-first**: Smoke detectors have been mandatory in Czechia for buildings approved after 2008 under Government Decree 23/2008 Sb., with an additional detector required if a flat's floor area exceeds 150 m²; older buildings generally aren't required to retrofit them.
- Cover: what this means practically for a rented flat (check if the building predates the rule, install one yourself regardless since they're cheap and it's your safety), where they typically go (kitchen, near boilers, bedroom).

### H2: The small setup details that get missed (300–400 words)
- **Answer-first**: A handful of small tasks cause disproportionate friction if skipped in week one: labelling the mailbox, registering for waste collection information, checking the building's house rules, and knowing your building's district-heating supplier before summer.
- Cover: the mailbox nameplate issue specifically (Czech Post won't deliver to, or accept a complaint about, an unmarked box), a pointer to the [hot water outages guide](/hot-water-outages-prague-2026) for the summer district-heating schedule, checking who to contact for repairs versus what's your own minor-repair responsibility (link to the [landlord vs tenant repairs guide](/apartment-maintenance-landlord-vs-yours-2026)).

### FAQ (4–5)
1. Do I have to register my address as a foreigner in Czechia? — Yes, within 3 working days, unless your landlord already filed it via UBYPORT (Police of the Czech Republic); missing it risks a fine up to 3,000 CZK.
2. What is a handover protocol and do I need one? — A written record of meter readings, serial numbers, and the flat's condition at move-in, signed by both parties; it's what protects you from billing and damage disputes later.
3. How long does it take to transfer electricity and gas into my name? — Typically 10 to 30 days, so start in week one rather than waiting.
4. Do I need a smoke detector in my flat? — Legally required only in buildings approved after 2008 (Government Decree 23/2008 Sb.); not required retroactively in older buildings, but cheap and worth installing regardless.
5. Why won't my mail arrive? — Czech Post will not deliver to, or take a delivery complaint about, a mailbox without your name on it; label it in week one.
