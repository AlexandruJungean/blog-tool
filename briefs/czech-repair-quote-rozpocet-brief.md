## Key Takeaways Draft
> **Key Takeaways**
> - A proper itemized quote (položkový rozpočet) separates material and labor costs line by line, with quantities and unit prices, not a single lump sum (ceskestavby.cz, 2026)
> - Home repair and reconstruction work usually qualifies for the reduced 12% VAT rate rather than the standard 21%, ask if that's reflected (ainvoice.cz, taxorio.cz, 2026)
> - An informal "odhad" (estimate) isn't binding, only a confirmed order or signed offer legally locks in the price, and that price must be final, including all taxes (podnikatel.cz, vlastnici.cz, 2026)
> - A quote without a stated validity date is technically open-ended, look for a clear expiry, typically 7 to 30 days (vyfakturuj.cz, 2026)

### H2: What should a proper itemized quote actually include? (300-350 words)
- Answer-first: a proper položkový rozpočet lists each item with a code, description, unit, quantity, and unit price, with material and labor shown separately, not folded into one number (ceskestavby.cz, 2026)
- Cover: why itemization protects you (you can query one line without disputing the whole quote), what a vague one-line "repair, 15,000 CZK" quote is missing
- **Image**: a written estimate or quote document on a table

### H2: Is the estimate you received actually legally binding? (300-350 words)
- Answer-first: an informal "odhad" isn't binding, it's just a rough indication, while a confirmed order or signed offer becomes a binding price the provider can't later raise, and that price must already include all taxes (podnikatel.cz, vlastnici.cz, 2026)
- Cover: how to tell which one you have (does it say "odhad" or "cenová nabídka" / "nabídka objednávky"), why this distinction matters if the final bill comes in higher than expected
- **Infographic 1**: simple two-column comparison, odhad vs. závazná nabídka

### H2: Which VAT rate should apply to your repair? (300-350 words)
- Answer-first: reconstruction and repair work on residential buildings usually qualifies for the reduced 12% VAT rate rather than the standard 21%, with no size limit for repairs to existing housing (ainvoice.cz, taxorio.cz, 2026)
- Cover: when 21% legitimately applies instead (non-residential work, oversized new builds), why it's worth asking directly if the quoted VAT rate looks off
- **Key stat**: 12% vs 21% with source

### H2: How long is a price quote actually valid? (250-300 words)
- Answer-first: a quote without a stated validity date is technically open-ended and unreliable, most legitimate quotes state a validity window of 7 to 30 days (vyfakturuj.cz, pajskr.cz, 2026)
- Cover: why this matters for material-price-sensitive jobs, what to do if a quote has no date at all
- **Infographic 2**: a short "before you agree" checklist (itemized, VAT rate stated, validity date, provider's IČO listed)

### H2: What are the red flags in a Czech repair quote? (250-300 words)
- Answer-first: the clearest red flags are a single unexplained lump sum, no VAT breakdown, and no validity date, all things a legitimate provider includes without being asked
- Cover: what to do if you spot one (ask for an itemized version before agreeing, don't be shy about it), when it's worth getting a second quote
