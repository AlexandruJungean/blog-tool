September is by far the busiest month for moving flats in Czechia, and it's no coincidence. It's the direct result of a legal notice period that most tradespeople never plan around, they just react to it once the phone starts ringing.

Between one tenant leaving and the next moving in, there's a narrow window of work, repainting, cleaning, a lock change, small repairs, that all has to happen within a few days. Whoever reaches out to landlords and property managers ahead of time catches the whole wave. Whoever waits gets whatever's left over.

## Why is September the peak of moving season?

Standard rental notice in Czechia is three months by law, and it always ends on the last calendar day of a month, never on an arbitrary date (Act No. 89/2012 Coll., Civil Code, §710). A notice given any time in June puts the move-out date at September 30. That mechanic, not chance, is why moves in Czechia cluster so heavily around September.

Two other forces add to it. The academic year starts in September, and with it over 330,547 university students move nationwide, up 5% year over year (Czech Statistical Office, end of 2025). And a large share of the foreign nationals living in Czechia, over 1.09 million people with registered residence (Czech Statistical Office, end of 2024), often move in the same window around new jobs or studies starting.

## What tenant turnover actually means as work

Between one tenant moving out and the next moving in, there's usually only a few days' window to get repainting, a move-out clean, a lock check or replacement, and small repairs done, scuffs, loose handles, checking appliances still work.

Who typically pays for what varies by job, and it's worth knowing before you pitch. Repainting and lock changes between tenants are usually paid by the landlord, as an investment in the next tenancy. Move-out cleaning, on the other hand, is often paid by the outgoing tenant, as part of returning the flat in the agreed condition. A pitch that respects that distinction reads as more credible than a blanket offer sent without context.

## What you can charge for a "tenant turnover" package

The individual line items have publicly findable prices, but almost nobody offers them as one package. Repainting a full flat of roughly 70 m² runs 30,000–45,000 CZK, roughly 70–180 CZK/m² depending on scope and finish quality. A move-out clean for a 2+1 or 3+1 flat runs 2,000–4,000 CZK. Replacing a cylinder lock starts around 490 CZK for the call-out plus 200–2,000 CZK depending on security grade.

## When and how to reach out to landlords and property managers

Outreach should start in July or August, not in September itself. That's when landlords are already receiving notices ending September 30, and exactly when they start looking for someone to turn the flat around.

## FAQ

**When do most people move in Czechia?**

Most heavily in September, driven by the 3-month notice period ending on the last calendar day of the month (Act No. 89/2012 Coll., §710) and the start of the academic year, when hundreds of thousands of students relocate.

**Does the lock have to be changed after a tenant moves out?**

It's not a legal requirement, but it's common good practice for security reasons between tenancies, especially if the previous tenant held their own keys to the flat.

**How much does it cost to repaint a flat between tenants?**

Roughly 70–180 CZK/m², so a 70 m² flat runs about 30,000–45,000 CZK depending on scope and finish quality.

**How far ahead should I reach out to landlords and property managers?**

Ideally six to eight weeks before the peak, so July to August, when landlords are already receiving notices ending in September.
