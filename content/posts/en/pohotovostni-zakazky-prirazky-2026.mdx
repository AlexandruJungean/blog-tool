## Do you have to follow Labor Code surcharge rules as self-employed?

No. Labor Code night and weekend surcharges, 10% to 25% depending on the sector, apply to employees, not to self-employed people pricing their own service based on the market (dostupnyadvokat.cz, e15.cz, 2026). This is probably the single most confused point in the whole topic.

![Tradesperson with tools by a van in the evening](https://images.pexels.com/photos/9679179/pexels-photo-9679179.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

The Labor Code exists to protect employees from an employer forcing night or weekend work without compensation. As a self-employed tradesperson, you're nobody's employee, you're selling your own service at a price you set yourself. No legal cap or minimum applies to your surcharge.

---

## What do other providers actually charge for night and weekend call-outs?

A standard call-out in Prague runs around 690 CZK, with an added surcharge for evening (4pm to 8am), weekend, and holiday work (A.K. Servis, 2026). Some providers charge a minimum call-out and basic service fee around 2,250 CZK for emergency work (Rychlá Pohotovost, 2026).

The spread between providers is wide, and that's fine, everyone has different costs and availability. What works as a reference point is a standard price plus a clearly defined surcharge, not a multiplier improvised on the spot based on how badly the customer needs help right now.

## How do you set your own surcharge fairly?

The simplest approach is three clear bands, standard hours, evening and weekend, and night, each with a fixed surcharge stated in advance, not estimated on the spot. Fixed bands read as far more trustworthy than anything that sounds improvised.

When setting the amount, factor in the real cost of being available, interrupted sleep, the risk of working in the dark or worse conditions, and having fewer options to walk away if a job turns out more complicated than it sounded on the phone.

## How do you explain the surcharge without it feeling like price gouging?

The biggest source of complaints isn't the surcharge amount, it's customers finding out about it only after you've arrived, not beforehand. This is entirely avoidable.

![Close-up of a wrench on a workbench](https://images.pexels.com/photos/8488058/pexels-photo-8488058.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

State your pricing visibly in your profile before a customer even reaches out. Confirm the price by message before you head out, not once you're on site. And explain the difference between the call-out fee and the actual repair cost, a lot of confusion comes from customers mixing the two up.

---

## Does it even pay to offer emergency service?

For trades like plumbing, electrical work, or locksmithing, yes, because emergencies don't wait for business hours, and a customer in genuine trouble is willing to pay for a fast, reliable response. It's not equally worthwhile for every trade, and it doesn't have to suit every tradesperson.

Be honest with yourself about your own capacity. Advertising emergency availability you then don't answer at night damages trust more than never offering it at all. It's better to offer emergency service only where you can genuinely deliver on it.

## FAQ

**How much should I charge for a night call-out?**

**How do I explain a surcharge without it feeling like price gouging?**
