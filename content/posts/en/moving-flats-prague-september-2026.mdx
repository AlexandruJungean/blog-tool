## Why is September Prague's busiest moving month?

Rental demand in Prague rises sharply once summer ends, and listings move fast, roughly a third are gone within three days of being posted (Bezrealitky data, via Prague Monitor and expats.cz reporting). Two things drive this at once: the academic year starting, which pulls students and staff into the city all at the same time, and a wave of leases ending on the same legal cycle.

That second part is the one almost nobody explains clearly. Czech leases don't end whenever a tenant feels like leaving, they end on a date set months in advance by the notice a tenant or landlord gave. Because so many notices get delivered around the same point in the year (often tied to the end of a fixed term, or simply because June is when people start thinking seriously about a move), the resulting move-out dates cluster heavily around September.

## The legal timeline: when to give notice and what it means for your move date

Standard notice to end a Czech lease is three months, and it always runs to the last calendar day of a month, not to a fixed date exactly 90 days later (Civil Code §710(2)). Give notice at any point in June, and September 30 is your earliest legal move-out date. Give notice in July instead, and you're looking at October 31.

## Real costs: what moving in Prague actually costs in 2026

A local Prague apartment move, movers only, typically runs 3,000–15,000 CZK depending on the size of the flat and how much needs to be carried (TraderPoint, May 2026). The national average cost of a move in Czechia is now around 7,120 CZK, up 8% year over year (expats.cz, Jul 2025), and Prague sits at the top of that range, with local hourly rates running as high as 1,600 CZK/hour at the busiest providers (expats.cz, Jul 2025).

## Who to book, and in what order

The sequence matters more in September than any other month, because movers specifically get booked out fast during the surge. Once you have a confirmed move-out date from your landlord, here's the order that avoids the most common scramble:

1. **Confirm the date in writing** with your landlord, referencing the notice you gave and the resulting legal move-out date.
2. **Book movers first**, ideally 2–3 weeks earlier than you would in a quieter month. Verify any company's IČO (business registration number) and ask about insurance before paying a deposit, this is the single most effective scam-avoidance step.
3. **Book move-out cleaning** next, timed for the day or two before handover.
4. **Schedule a handyman** for anything that needs fixing before the landlord's walkthrough, scuffed walls, a loose fixture, a dripping tap.
5. **Transfer utilities and internet in parallel** with the above, not after you've already moved, since transfers take real lead time.

## Move-out cleaning and handyman fixes before handover

A thorough move-out clean and any small repairs handled before handover are what actually gets your deposit back in full, not an argument with your landlord after the fact. Photograph everything once it's clean and fixed, before the landlord's own walkthrough, so there's a timestamped record if anything is disputed later.

Common pre-handover fixes worth budgeting for: a scuffed wall that needs a touch-up, a dripping tap, a loose door handle or cabinet hinge, checking that appliances you're required to leave in working order actually are. ## Utilities, internet and address registration

Start transferring electricity, gas and internet the same week you book your movers, not after you've already moved in. Electricity and gas in Prague typically run through ČEZ or PRE, and internet through providers like O2 or Vodafone, plan for the transfer to take one to two weeks rather than assuming it happens instantly (no single verified figure exists for exact processing times, so treat this as a planning buffer rather than a guarantee).

If you're a foreign national, there's a separate legal deadline to know about: you're required to register your new address with the Police of the Czech Republic within 3 working days of moving in, unless your landlord has already filed it electronically on your behalf through the UBYPORT system. ## FAQ

**How much notice do I need to give to end a lease in Prague?**

Three months, running to the last calendar day of a month, under Civil Code §710(2). Notice given in June, for example, makes September 30 your earliest legal move-out date.

**How much does it cost to move flat in Prague?**

A local move typically runs 3,000–15,000 CZK for movers alone (TraderPoint, 2026), with the national average around 7,120 CZK (expats.cz, 2025). Add roughly 2,500–4,500 CZK for cleaning and 400–800 CZK/hour for any handyman work.

**When is the best time to move apartment in Prague?**

Outside September if you have the flexibility, since that's when demand and listing turnover both peak. If September is your only option, book movers 2–3 weeks earlier than you would in a quieter month.

**Do I get my deposit back when I move out in Czech Republic?**

Generally yes, minus any provable deductions for unpaid rent or documented damage beyond normal wear and tear. See our full [deposit return guide](/security-deposits-prague-move-out-2026) for the details.
