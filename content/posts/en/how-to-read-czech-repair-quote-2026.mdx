A quote can look perfectly professional, a nice document, a logo, a total at the bottom, and still be a single lump sum hiding no actual breakdown. That's the part most people miss until the final bill comes in higher than expected.

Czech repair quotes come in two flavors that aren't interchangeable: a rough odhad and a binding rozpočet or nabídka. Knowing which one you're holding changes what you can actually do if something goes wrong.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - A proper itemized quote (položkový rozpočet) separates material and labor costs line by line, not a single lump sum (ceskestavby.cz, 2026)
> - Home repair and reconstruction work usually qualifies for the reduced 12% VAT rate rather than the standard 21%, ask if that's reflected (ainvoice.cz, taxorio.cz, 2026)
> - An informal "odhad" isn't binding, only a confirmed order or signed offer legally locks in the price, and that price must already include all taxes (podnikatel.cz, vlastnici.cz, 2026)
> - A quote without a stated validity date is technically open-ended, look for a clear expiry, typically 7 to 30 days (vyfakturuj.cz, 2026)

## What should a proper itemized quote actually include?

A proper položkový rozpočet lists each item with a code, description, unit, quantity, and unit price, with material and labor shown separately, not folded into one number (ceskestavby.cz, 2026). That structure exists for a reason, it's what lets you actually verify what you're paying for.

![Hand holding a company invoice on a clipboard with a pen](https://images.pexels.com/photos/7651555/pexels-photo-7651555.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

A vague one-line quote, "bathroom repair, 15,000 CZK," tells you nothing about what's included. Is that just labor? Does it cover the pipe if it needs replacing too? An itemized version lets you query one line without disputing the entire quote, which matters a lot if the final bill doesn't match.

## Is the estimate you received actually legally binding?

An informal "odhad" isn't binding, it's a rough indication, not a promise. A confirmed order or a signed offer, on the other hand, becomes a binding price the provider can't later raise, and that price must already include all applicable taxes (podnikatel.cz, [vlastnici.cz](http://www.vlastnici.cz/jaky-je-rozdil-mezi-zavaznou-a-nezavaznou-cenovou-nabidkou/), 2026).

If a document just says "odhad" or was given verbally, treat the final number as approximate. If it's a confirmed order, a signed nabídka, or something you formally accepted, that price is what you owe, nothing more, unless the scope of work genuinely changed.

## Which VAT rate should apply to your repair?

Reconstruction and repair work on residential buildings usually qualifies for the reduced 12% VAT rate rather than the standard 21%, and for repairs to existing housing this reduced rate applies without the size limits that apply to new construction ([ainvoice.cz](https://ainvoice.cz/blog/sazby-dph-2026/), [taxorio.cz](https://www.taxorio.cz/blog/sazby-dph-2026/), 2026).

The 21% rate legitimately applies to non-residential work or oversized new builds, but for an ordinary flat or house repair, 12% is what you should typically see. If a quote shows 21% for a straightforward apartment repair, it's worth asking directly why.

## How long is a price quote actually valid?

A quote without a stated validity date is technically open-ended and less trustworthy for it, most legitimate quotes state a validity window of 7 to 30 days ([vyfakturuj.cz](https://www.vyfakturuj.cz/slovnik-pojmu/cenova-nabidka/), [pajskr.cz](https://pajskr.cz/jak-napsat-cenovou-nabidku/), 2026). Isn't it strange how often that one detail gets left off entirely?

This matters most for jobs where material prices move, a quote from two months ago for a job with volatile material costs isn't something you can necessarily hold a provider to. If your quote has no date at all, ask for one before agreeing.

## What are the red flags in a Czech repair quote?

The clearest red flags are a single unexplained lump sum, no VAT breakdown at all, and no validity date, all things a legitimate provider includes without being asked twice. None of these alone proves bad intent, but together they're worth pausing over.

If you spot one, simply ask for an itemized version before agreeing to anything, a provider who genuinely stands behind their pricing won't mind the question. ## FAQ

**What's the difference between an odhad and a cenová nabídka?**

An odhad is an informal, non-binding estimate. A cenová nabídka, once confirmed or signed, is a binding price the provider can't later raise ([podnikatel.cz](https://www.podnikatel.cz/zakony/zakon-c-526-1990-sb-o-cenach/f1339941/), 2026).

**What VAT rate should I see on a home repair quote?**

Usually 12%, the reduced rate for residential repair and reconstruction, rather than the standard 21% ([ainvoice.cz](https://ainvoice.cz/blog/sazby-dph-2026/), 2026). Ask directly if you see 21% on an ordinary flat repair.

**How long should a quote stay valid?**

Typically 7 to 30 days, stated explicitly on the document. If no date is given, treat the quote as unreliable and ask for one ([vyfakturuj.cz](https://www.vyfakturuj.cz/slovnik-pojmu/cenova-nabidka/), 2026).

**Can a Czech provider charge more than the quoted price?**

Only if the quote was an informal odhad, not a confirmed binding offer. Once you've accepted a formal nabídka, that price stands unless the actual scope of work changes.
