Turning down a job hurts twice. Once immediately, when you tell the customer no. And again a few weeks later, when you find out they found someone else for good.

If that keeps happening, the problem isn't demand. It's capacity. And that's where most tradespeople ask the wrong first question, usually "should I hire someone," when the real question is which of several very different options actually fits.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - A DPP helper agreement caps at 11,999 CZK a month per employer in 2026 before social and health insurance kick in, with a 300-hour annual limit (Asociace občanských poraden, mzdy.cz, 2026)
> - A cooperating person, typically a spouse, can take a share of both income and expenses, but must be registered with the tax office, social security, and health insurer (Portál POHODA, 2026)
> - A first real employee costs more than the gross wage alone, employers pay an extra 24.8% social and 9% health insurance on top (ČSOB Průvodce podnikáním, 2026)
> - Subcontracting with another self-employed tradesperson works well for one-off overflow or a specific skill, with no employer registration required

## How do you know it's time to stop doing everything yourself?

The clearest sign is turning down work repeatedly, not once a month, but regularly, because your schedule is booked out for weeks. The second sign is administrative work eating into hours you'd rather spend on paid jobs.

If you're declining work while knowing you could take on more with help, whether that's an extra pair of hands on site or someone handling paperwork, the real question isn't whether to get help. It's which kind fits what you actually need.

## What is a DPP, and is it the easiest way to bring on help?

A DPP (dohoda o provedení práce) caps at 11,999 CZK a month per employer in 2026, below that threshold you don't pay social or health insurance, and the person can work a maximum of 300 hours a year for you (Asociace občanských poraden, mzdy.cz, 2026).

The minimum hourly wage for 2026 is 134.40 CZK. A DPP is the simplest way to bring on a helper for supporting tasks, carrying materials, prep work, simpler jobs, without becoming a full employer with monthly insurance filings and a registered payroll.

## Can a family member help without becoming an employee?

Yes, through the "cooperating person" arrangement, typically a spouse, or a parent or child sharing your household, who can take a share of both business income and expenses (Portál POHODA, 2026). Czech labour law doesn't let a self-employed person formally employ their own spouse, which is exactly why this arrangement exists.

The condition is that the person genuinely participates in the business, handling bookings, customer communication, or invoicing, not just on paper. You must register the arrangement with the tax office, the social security administration, and the health insurer, and the cooperating person gets their own tax ID.

For 2026, minimum monthly contributions are 5,720 CZK for social insurance and 3,306 CZK for health insurance for the main activity, unless the cooperating person is already covered another way, such as through separate employment or parental leave (Portál POHODA, dumfinanci.cz, 2026).

## When does it make sense to hire a real employee instead?

A real employee on a work contract makes sense once you need steady, predictable capacity, not occasional help. The costs run noticeably higher than most tradespeople expect, employers pay an extra 24.8% social and 9% health insurance on top of the gross wage (ČSOB Průvodce podnikáním, 2026).

The paperwork adds up too. You must register with the ČSSZ employer registry within 8 days of the employee's start date, and a signed work contract is required no later than their first day (superkariera.cz, jake-james.cz, 2026). Before taking this step, it's worth checking whether a DPP or a subcontracting arrangement would cover the same need for less.

## Or should you just subcontract with another tradesperson?

Subcontracting with another self-employed tradesperson fits exactly the situations above, one-off overflow, or a specific skill you don't have yourself, an electrician for a plumbing job, a painter after your drywall work. No employer registration is needed, each side handles their own insurance and tax.

The downside is obvious: you don't have the same direct control as with an employee, and reliability depends on how well you can count on each other. For short-term overflow, though, it's often the fastest and cheapest of the four options.

## Does it pay to specialize, or stay a generalist?

As demand grows, this question follows close behind: keep taking everything that comes in, or focus on what pays best and what you do best? Specializing usually means a higher hourly rate, faster training for any helper you bring on, and a clearer profile that's easier for customers to understand at a glance.

Staying a generalist means more opportunities, since you can say yes to a wider range of requests, but harder delegation, since a helper needs to learn more different tasks at once. Neither option is universally better, it depends on whether you want to grow deeper or wider.

## FAQ

**Do I have to pay insurance for a helper on a DPP?**

Only if their monthly pay from you goes above 11,999 CZK, below that threshold in 2026 neither social nor health insurance is due. They also can't work more than 300 hours a year for you.

**Can my spouse help without me employing them formally?**

Yes, as a cooperating person, as long as they genuinely participate in the business. You need to register it with the tax office, social security administration, and health insurer, and you transfer a matching share of income and expenses to them.

**How much extra will a first employee actually cost me?**

On top of the gross wage, you pay an extra 24.8% for social insurance and 9% for health insurance as the employer. Total cost ends up noticeably higher than the wage figure alone suggests.

**Is it better to specialize or take on any kind of job?**

It depends on whether you want to grow deeper, with a higher rate and a clearer profile, or wider, with more requests but harder delegation to a helper. Both are legitimate strategies, they just lead in different directions.
