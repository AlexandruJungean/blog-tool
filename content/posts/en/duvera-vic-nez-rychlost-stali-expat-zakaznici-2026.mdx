A fast reply wins you the first job. What wins you the tenth?

Many tradespeople optimise for one thing: answering quickly. It matters, and it's genuinely how you get picked for a first booking. But speed alone doesn't explain why a customer calls the same person again six months later, or why they mention that person's name in an expat group without being asked. That's a different mechanism, and it's worth understanding on its own.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - A 5% increase in customer retention can raise profit by 25 to 95% (Bain & Company, via Harvard Business Review), because keeping a customer is cheaper and more valuable than winning a new one
> - An existing customer has roughly a 60 to 70% chance of buying again, compared to just 5 to 20% for a new prospect
> - Speed wins the booking. Trust, built across five concrete moments, wins the relationship
> - Many expats rely more heavily on a provider's profile, reviews, and personal experience than on a wide local network, so one satisfied expat customer can bring several more

---

## Why is a repeat customer worth more than a new job?

A 5% increase in customer retention can raise profit by 25 to 95% (Fred Reichheld / Bain & Company, via Harvard Business Review). An existing customer also has roughly a 60 to 70% chance of buying again, compared to just 5 to 20% for someone who has never used you before.

![A mechanic and a customer shaking hands after a job is completed](https://images.pexels.com/photos/6720550/pexels-photo-6720550.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

That math holds for a single tradesperson just as much as it does for a large company. Constantly chasing new customers costs more time and energy than looking after the ones you already have, and a customer who already trusts you is far cheaper to keep than a stranger is to convince.

---

## Speed opens the door, but it doesn't keep you inside

A fast first reply decides who gets picked for the initial job, see our guide on [how to get more jobs as a tradesperson](/jak-ziskat-vic-zakazek-remeslnik-2026) for the mechanics of that. On its own, though, speed says nothing about whether the customer comes back.

A provider who answers within the hour but shows up late, communicates poorly, or leaves the price as a surprise at the end will still lose that customer to someone else next time. Speed is the entry ticket. Trust is what decides whether that customer books you again, or quietly tries someone new.

## Why trust matters even more with expat customers

Many expats don't have as wide a circle of Czech contacts to ask for a recommendation, so they lean more heavily on a provider's profile, reviews, and their own direct experience than on a network of neighbours and family. That makes the five moments above carry extra weight with this group specifically.

It also means the payoff compounds differently. One satisfied expat customer often brings several more, because expat communities share recommendations actively in groups and forums. Referred customers convert roughly four times more often and stick around notably longer than customers who found you cold, so a single good experience genuinely ripples outward.

## What aftercare actually looks like

A short message after the job is finished, and one seasonal check-in before the next busy period, does more for retention than another discount ever will. Neither takes long, and both are easy to build into a routine.

Keep a simple list: customer name, address, what you did, and the date. A spreadsheet or even a notebook is enough. Once a year, before the season that matches the work you do, send a short message, "getting ready for [season], let me know if you'd like your [system] checked." That single message reminds a satisfied customer you exist right when they're about to need you again. ## FAQ

**Is a fast reply enough to keep a customer long-term?**

No. A fast reply decides who gets the first job. Whether that customer comes back depends on trust built during the work itself and the communication around it, not on response time alone.

**How much does it actually pay to focus on retention?**

A 5% increase in customer retention can raise profit by 25 to 95% (Bain & Company, via Harvard Business Review). Keeping an existing customer is consistently cheaper than winning a new one, which makes retention worth deliberate effort even for a solo tradesperson.

**Why are expat customers more sensitive to trust signals?**

Many don't have as wide a circle of Czech contacts to ask for recommendations, so they rely more on a provider's profile, reviews, and clear communication than on a personal referral network.

**Do I need to speak English to keep these customers?**

No. On Tool Connect, your Czech profile and messages are translated automatically into English, and the customer's English messages come back to you in Czech. The translation runs both directions.

**What's a simple way to start with aftercare?**
